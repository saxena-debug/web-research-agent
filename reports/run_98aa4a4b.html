Research Report: Top 5 Teams with the Best Chances to Win the FIFA World Cup 2026

Executive Summary

The FIFA World Cup 2026 is shaping up to be a highly competitive tournament with several teams emerging as strong contenders. Spain, France, Argentina, England, and Portugal are frequently highlighted across multiple sources as the top teams with the best chances of winning. Spain is often identified as the favorite, bolstered by recent successes and a strong squad. France and Argentina, both with recent World Cup finals appearances, are also considered formidable opponents. England and Portugal round out the top five, each with a mix of historical success and current squad strength. While there is consensus on these teams' potential, the exact rankings and probabilities vary across different predictive models and analyses.

Key Findings

  • Spain: Frequently ranked as the top contender, Spain is noted for its high Elo rating and recent success, including a UEFA EURO 2024 victory. The Goldman Sachs model assigns Spain a 26% chance of winning, while the Opta supercomputer gives them a 16.02% chance. Spain's squad is highlighted for its scoring talent and momentum.
  • France: Consistently ranked among the top contenders, France is given a 19% chance by the Goldman Sachs model and a 12.54% chance by the Opta supercomputer. France's recent history includes a World Cup win in 2018 and a runner-up finish in 2022, with Kylian Mbappe as a key player.
  • Argentina: As the defending champions, Argentina is seen as a strong contender but faces a "winner's slump" effect, reducing their chances to 14% according to Goldman Sachs and 10.09% per the Opta supercomputer. Lionel Messi's leadership remains a crucial factor.
  • England: England is aiming to overcome historical underperformance, with a 10.66% chance according to the Opta supercomputer and a lower 5% chance by Goldman Sachs. England's squad quality and management under Thomas Tuchel are noted strengths.
  • Portugal: Portugal is pursuing its first World Cup victory, with a 6.92% chance according to the Opta supercomputer. Their squad strength and ambition are recognized, though they are often ranked lower than the other top contenders.
  • Discrepancies: Notable differences exist in the rankings and probabilities assigned by different sources, particularly regarding Brazil and Germany, which are sometimes mentioned as strong contenders outside the top five.

Analysis

Spain emerges as the consensus favorite to win the FIFA World Cup 2026, consistently ranked at the top across various sources. This is largely attributed to their recent successes, including a UEFA EURO 2024 victory, and a strong squad featuring young talents like Lamine Yamal, Pedri, and Fermín López. Spain's high Elo rating and scoring capabilities further bolster their position as a top contender. The consistent high ranking across multiple predictive models underscores Spain's potential to reclaim the World Cup title.

France is another team frequently highlighted as a strong contender, with a history of success in recent World Cups. Their depth and emerging young talent, led by Kylian Mbappe, make them a formidable opponent. The Goldman Sachs model and the Opta supercomputer both assign France a significant chance of winning, reflecting their potential to capitalize on their recent runner-up finish in 2022 and their victory in 2018.

Argentina, as the defending champions, faces the challenge of overcoming the "winner's slump" effect, which historically sees defending champions underperform in subsequent tournaments. Despite this, Argentina remains a top contender, with Lionel Messi's leadership and the team's recent World Cup victory providing a strong foundation. The varied probabilities assigned by different models highlight the uncertainty surrounding Argentina's ability to defend their title.

England's potential to win the World Cup is tempered by historical underperformance and geographical challenges. Despite these factors, England's strong squad and management under Thomas Tuchel provide a solid basis for optimism. The discrepancies in probabilities assigned by different models reflect the ongoing debate about England's ability to break their long-standing World Cup drought.

Portugal's pursuit of their first World Cup triumph is supported by a strong squad and high ambitions. While often ranked lower than the other top contenders, Portugal's consistent presence in the top five across multiple sources indicates their potential to make a significant impact in the tournament. The lower probabilities assigned by some models suggest that while Portugal is a strong contender, they may face challenges in overcoming more historically successful teams.

Overall, while Spain, France, and Argentina are consistently highlighted as the top contenders, the exact rankings and probabilities vary across different sources. This reflects the inherent unpredictability of the World Cup and the multitude of factors that can influence a team's chances of success. The presence of other strong teams like Brazil and Germany outside the top five further underscores the competitive nature of the tournament, suggesting that the 2026 World Cup will be a closely contested event.
